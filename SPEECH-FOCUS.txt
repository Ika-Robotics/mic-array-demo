IKA -- FIXED MOUTH TARGET / GEOMETRY-BASED AUDIO FOCUS

START
Close other microphone demos, then double-click Start Speech Focus.cmd in File
Explorer. Start Demo.cmd now opens this same view. No room measurement, phone
tone, alignment captures or saved calibration profile is needed.

1. Wait for live external video and the 16-channel array.
2. Click your MOUTH in the video. The cyan marker stays at that image location.
3. Click Record comparison. A three-second countdown precedes ten seconds of
   audio recording. Keep your mouth at the point and speak normally; the crowd
   can continue. Moving the target/geometry is locked during recording.
4. Click Play original, then Play focused. Keep your playback volume the same.
5. Open comparison files if you want to inspect or share the clips.

There is no automatic face tracking. Click again after you move. An orange
marker, when enabled, indicates the strongest sound; it does not steer focus.
It defaults off and pauses during recording to reduce processing.
The cyan mouth target steers it even if someone elsewhere is louder.
The panel shows "Mouth target: LOCKED" after selection. Only a new mouth click
or Clear mouth target changes that selection during the session. Device errors
and camera changes keep the point; a real interruption can still stop recording
with an explanation. Check the point if the camera itself moves or changes.

COMPARISON FILES
Each completed recording creates a new timestamped folder under recordings/:
  original.wav: microphone 1, unfiltered (with common processing delay).
  focused.wav: all 16 microphones delayed and averaged toward your mouth,
               then filtered to 150-3800 Hz.
  reference-speech-band.wav: microphone 1 with that same frequency filter.
  settings.json: selected point, geometry, devices, algorithm and playback gain.

All files are mono PCM16 at 48 kHz, ten seconds of received samples. Gaps can
extend capture time and create discontinuities; settings.json records them. They use ONE common
playback gain based on the largest peak across all three files. They are not
normalized independently. Compare Focused against Speech-band reference to
hear direction filtering separately from the removal of low/high frequencies.

Only pressing Record writes real audio. The countdown and cancelled captures
are not saved. No video or full 16-channel recording is saved. Playback stops
before the countdown; there is no live audio feedback/monitoring. Microphone
capture runs independently of processing. Recoverable overruns and clipping
show an amber warning and let recording continue. A stalled camera preview
does not stop audio or change the fixed direction. A microphone error or no
fresh microphone samples for three seconds stops recording with a persistent
red banner. focus-events.log preserves recording events and stop reasons.
Settings include input_overflow_events, processing_queue_dropped_blocks,
clipped_blocks, quality_warning, capture_wall_seconds and duration_basis.

GEOMETRY
Defaults match the initial demo: 4x4 array with 42 mm microphone spacing,
80-degree assumed horizontal camera FOV, centered camera pointing forward,
no flips and no rotation. Actual video aspect ratio sets vertical FOV.
FOV -/+ and Rotate/Flip controls let you correct an obvious mismatch without
sound calibration. These are assumptions, not a measured camera calibration.
Keep the camera centered and rigidly aligned with the array. The target and
manual adjustments reset on restart; saved wizard profiles are not modified.

WHAT THIS DOES
Fractional delay-and-sum beamforming emphasizes the selected direction. A
speech-band filter removes rumble and high-frequency sound. There is no room
noise gate, so a loud crowd cannot block capture of the selected direction.

It does not identify your voice or prove your mouth is speaking. Nearby voices
at the same angle, low-frequency sound and room reflections may remain. A flat
array also has front/back ambiguity. At close distances, camera lens/FOV error
and the far-field assumption can reduce accuracy. The marker is not a tight
acoustic boundary around the mouth. Listen to the comparison before judging
whether this is sufficient for Ika; real-world separation is not yet validated.

IKA
ika-focus-status.json is a separate local geometry/target status file. It does
not carry audio. calibrated=false and speech/person-speaking remain unknown.
No Ika service is changed or connected. See IKA-INTEGRATION.txt for details.

BUILD / CHECKS
build.cmd produces MicArrayFocus-Buffered.exe (default: speech focus).
  MicArrayFocus-Buffered.exe --focus-tests
  MicArrayFocus-Buffered.exe --render-focus-tests
  MicArrayFocus-Buffered.exe --self-test
Legacy wizard remains available with --wizard / Start Setup Wizard.cmd.
Tests use synthetic sources and write only under test-output.

REFERENCE
https://www.mathworks.com/help/audio/ug/acoustic-beamforming-using-a-microphone-array.html

LIVE CAPTURE CHECK
Close the demo, then run MicArrayFocus-Buffered.exe --focus-capture-check.
This opens the real array for 64 blocks, with additional simulated processing
delay, and reports overruns/drops. It saves and plays no audio.
